\section{Theory and Problem Formulation}
\label{sec:formulation}

\paragraph{MMOP} An MMOP minimises $F(x)=(f_1(x),\dots,f_m(x))$ over a decision space
$\Omega$. Distinct regions of $\Omega$ may map to the same Pareto front; the MMOP goal is
to approximate the Pareto front in objective space \emph{and} cover all of its
decision-space pre-images (the Pareto sets). We use Deb's constraint-domination, the
special crowding distance (SCD; the larger of objective- and decision-space crowding),
and standard real-coded variation.

\paragraph{Emergency-facility placement MMOP} As a real-map case study we place $K$
emergency-response stations to serve $M$ demand points sampled from a real OpenStreetMap (OSM)
city graph.
The decision vector encodes $K$ station positions in the city bounding box (snapped to
the nearest OSM node), $n_{\mathrm{var}}=2K$. The two objectives are the \emph{mean access
distance} $f_1=\tfrac{1}{M}\sum_d \min_k \mathrm{dist}(d,s_k)$ (the classical $p$-median
objective, average response efficiency) and the \emph{maximum access distance}
$f_2=\max_d \min_k \mathrm{dist}(d,s_k)$ (the $p$-centre objective, worst-case fairness);
both are foundational discrete facility-location
problems~\parencite{daskin2013facility,revelle2008location}, here cast jointly as a bi-objective
MMOP over a real street network. On a real city with several
districts, many geographically distinct layouts achieve near-identical $(f_1,f_2)$, i.e.\
genuine objective-space degeneracy yielding multiple \emph{Pareto-equivalent placement
families}, precisely the decision-space multimodality EARS-MMOEA targets. For
permutation-invariant decision-space comparison, each layout's $K$ stations are sorted
into a canonical order. This canonical-coordinate sort is a computationally trivial surrogate
for the exact set distance $\min_{\pi}\frac1K\sum_i \mathrm{dist}(a_i,b_{\pi(i)})$ (a $K\times K$
assignment, solvable in closed form for $K{=}5$); the two agree when stations are well separated
but can differ when layouts cross, so we treat the canonical sort as an approximation and note
the exact matching distance as a refinement for future work.
