\begin{abstract}
Computational design often requires several distinct solutions with comparable trade-offs, rather
than a single Pareto set. Emergency-facility placement is one example: station layouts can be
equivalent in mean and worst-case access while differing substantially in geography. Multimodal
multi-objective optimisation formalises this requirement, but standard selection pressure often
collapses equivalent decision regions. This study shows that the location of the decision-space
diversity signal is the key design choice. In a Pareto-based evolutionary algorithm, sparsity
should be used as a non-negative \emph{within-front} selection term and kept out of the dominance
sort. This separates convergence, carried by rank, from decision-space coverage, carried by
tie-breaking inside the splitting front. A controlled study that fixes the backbone and sparsity
signal shows that within-front placement outperforms in-sort fusion. Multiplicative and additive
within-front forms are statistically similar, indicating that placement rather than algebraic form
carries the effect. The principle is implemented in an equivalence-aware evolutionary framework
with archives, adaptive niching and an operator portfolio. Benchmarks, scalable families and a
deceptive counter-regime define where the method is effective. In a five-city OpenStreetMap
facility-placement case study, the method returns geographically distinct layouts with
near-equivalent access trade-offs. The method is not favoured when diversity comes from deceptive
local Pareto sets rather than equivalent global optima. All baselines, protocols, seeds and
statistics are released.
\end{abstract}

\textbf{Keywords}: multimodal multi-objective optimisation, evolutionary algorithm,
computational design, decision-space diversity, facility location, OpenStreetMap.
